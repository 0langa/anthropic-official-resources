## Key takeaways[](https://academy.claude.com/courses/ai-fluency-for-creative-work/description-and-discernment)

- AI cannot serve a practice you have not described, and you cannot evaluate output against standards you have not made explicit.
- Each layer of your practice, Product, Process, and Performance, runs its own Description and Discernment loop.
- Process standards live at the step level: that is what lets you judge whether AI at a given step served the work or undermined it.
- Performance here means your behavior as a working creator, and is the layer most affected by AI over time.

Product standards

#### Calibrate

Test your standards. Take an older piece of your own, ideally one you feel mixed about, and judge it against your Standards. If everything passes, the standards are flattery, not standards. Go back to your Standards and sharpen them.

Be specific. If nothing missed, your standards may be too soft.

## Transcript

The work in this module is articulation of your creative practice. This articulation process is crucial to determine if, when, and how AI should be incorporated into your creative work. Articulation is made up of two phases. First, Description: putting into words your creative practice using the three layers. Second, Discernment: building the standards you'll use to evaluate any work produced with or without AI.

Most creators have not articulated their own practice in detail. Even practitioners with decades of experience often have not put words to what they do, how they do it, or how they work. Some of this is because the work is intuitive. Some of it is because you do not need to articulate practice in order to do it. You just need to do it.

Here's where that breaks down with AI. Description: AI cannot serve a practice you have not described. When you ask AI to do something, you're describing what you want. Vague description produces generic output. Discernment: You cannot evaluate output against standards you have not made explicit. If you have not articulated your standards, or if they only exist as a gut feeling, you are more likely to accept whatever AI produces.

The articulation work covers three layers of your practice. Each one matters for different reasons, and each one shows up differently when AI enters the picture. Three questions drive product description. What do you make? Not the category, but the specifics. Not "Illustrations," but "Editorial illustrations, typically conceptual rather than representational, for print and digital magazine contexts." The more specific you are, the more useful the description becomes.

What makes it yours? Two children's book illustrators may seemingly illustrate the same subject, but the specific signatures of their work makes their product distinct. Your palette, your line quality, the way you handle emotional register, the combination of choices that makes your work recognizable across pieces. That's what you're trying to name.

What qualities define your best work? When something succeeds, what's present? When it falls short, what's missing? "It feels right" isn't specific enough to do any work here. "The figures have weight, and the negative space carries half the composition" is. "The essay earns its ending" is. Those are specific enough to evaluate against.

If a peer asked you to teach them to recognize excellence in your work, what would you point to? A quality you can teach is a quality you can apply to AI output. Without that articulation, "good enough" wins by default because it doesn't look wrong.

Process description is the sequence of how the work actually gets made with the complex and messy stages included. Where in your process do the most important decisions get made? For some creators, it's early. The concept stage frames everything and execution works out what's already decided. For others, the important decisions happen in revision, when the first version reveals what the work actually wants to be.

Where do you struggle? Every creator has stages that are hard. Naming the hard stages is part of describing the process honestly. What gets handed off and to whom? Other people are usually part of the process, even when the work is credited to a single author. Naming the handoffs surfaces where the work depends on others, which matters when you are thinking about AI use.

What feedback do you rely on, when and from whom? Some creators show drafts to a trusted reader early. Some show nothing until late. Some publish in installments and incorporate audience response. The feedback architecture of your process matters when thinking about how AI might offer feedback, or even substitute for human feedback.

And here's a pattern worth naming. When creators articulate the process honestly, two things show up that surprise them. The first is that the messy stages do more work than they realized. The second is that some of what feels like wasted time is actually very important. Both observations matter as you determine if, how, and where to leverage AI.

Process Discernment is about how work gets made. Discernment converts the inventory of your process, your actual stages of work, the tools used, the decision points where work actually gets made, into standards at the level of individual steps rather than the process as a whole. Each process step has its own success criteria, and understanding success at the individual step level improves your understanding of the whole process.

Here's why this matters for AI. When you know what a step done well looks like, you can evaluate whether AI involvement at that step served the work or undermined it. Without step-level standards, you're evaluating the whole process from the output end when it's too late. The standards that come out of this exercise are often more specific than creators expect. The vague version, "The work needs to be good" does no analytical work, while the specific version, "Frame the design so the foreground pops using neon colors and more graphical elements" does.

In this module, Performance Description is about your behavior as a working creator. The questions are about how you function as a working person in the moment of making, and across the longer arc of your practice. When are you at your best? When are you at your worst? And what patterns repeat?

When do you do your best work? Not just the time of day, but conditions, mood, what came before you started working? What does your best work-producing state feel like from the inside, and how do you tell whether you're in it? What does a stuck or compromised state look like for you? Hard days happen. Compromised performance is different. It's when something has actually gone wrong with your working process. The early signals tend to be specific and recognizable once you've named them.

How do you respond to feedback, to pressure, to isolation, to collaboration? AI tools tend to compress timelines and shift the solitude-to-collaboration ratio of your work. Whether that helps or hurts depends entirely on how you're built.

Performance Discernment is the standard that matters most for the long arc of your practice, and it's the one most directly affected by AI over time. What does sustained, good performance look like across a full project? What are the earliest signs of compromised performance? Outcome measures tell you whether something went wrong. Diagnostic signals tell you something's starting to go wrong while there's still time to do something about it.

This is also where skill atrophy shows up, the slow decline of execution skills that comes from not using them. Your ability to direct and discern AI's performance depends on understanding how you work. You can't direct what you can't recognize. Performance is also where the long-term concerns about AI and creative practice show up. The ways AI shifts your time, your working patterns, your feedback loops, and your relationship to difficulty are performance-level effects. What comes next relies on you having an articulated performance standard to work from.

So with all this in mind, your next step is to practice articulating your practice, as well as the evaluation standards for each part of it. Essentially, you'll start with Description and then set yourself up for clear Discernment. Let's get started.
