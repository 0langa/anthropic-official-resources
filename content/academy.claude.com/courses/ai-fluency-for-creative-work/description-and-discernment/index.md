## Key takeaways[](https://academy.claude.com/courses/ai-fluency-for-creative-work/description-and-discernment)

- AI cannot serve a practice you have not described, and you cannot evaluate output against standards you have not made explicit.
- Each layer of your practice, Product, Process, and Performance, runs its own Description and Discernment loop.
- Process standards live at the step level: that is what lets you judge whether AI at a given step served the work or undermined it.
- Performance here means your behavior as a working creator, and is the layer most affected by AI over time.

Two ground rules

- Be specific, not category-level. Not "illustrations," but "editorial illustrations for print magazines."
- No empty phrases. Skip "it feels right" or "you know it when you see it." The workbook flags them as you type.

#### Product: what you make

What do you make?

The specifics: form, context, audience, scope. Not the category.

What makes it yours?

The signatures that make your version recognizable: palette, rhythm, structural habits, emotional register.

What qualities define your best work?

What is present when a piece succeeds, and missing when it falls short?

#### Process: how you make it

Map your process one stage at a time. Add a row for each stage of your actual work, false starts and messy parts included, then note what is hard, where the decisions get made, and who else is involved.

#### Performance: how you work

Your rhythms

Your patterns across a day, week, project, and the longer arc. Where the real work happens.

Your best working state

When you do your best work: conditions, mood, what came before. How it feels from the inside.

Warning signs

Your earliest signals that something is going wrong, often before it shows in the work itself.

Pressure, solitude, collaboration

How you respond to each, and how AI tends to shift your timelines and solitude-to-collaboration balance.

#### Convert to standards

Turn each description into standards a peer who is not you could apply to work that is not yours.

Product standards

From Product: what makes work excellent, and how a peer tells adequate from excellent.

Process standards

From Process: for each major stage, what doing it well looks like.

Performance standards

From Performance: what sustained good performance looks like, and the signals that warn you early.

#### Calibrate

Test your standards. Take an older piece of your own, ideally one you feel mixed about, and judge it against your Standards. If everything passes, the standards are flattery, not standards. Go back to your Standards and sharpen them.

The piece you are testing

Name or briefly describe it.

Which standards did it meet?

Be specific.

Which did it miss?

Be specific. If nothing missed, your standards may be too soft.
