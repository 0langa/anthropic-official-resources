## Transcript

Choices about what to delegate to AI tend to collect around five outcomes. We'll explore each of them here. The five outcomes, No AI, AI as a consultant, AI as tool builder, AI as co-creator, and AI as medium. They split along one clear line, whether AI appears in the final work the audience encounters. No AI, consultant, and tool builder are outcomes where it doesn't. Co-creator and medium are outcomes where it does. The same creator working across different projects or different tasks within one project may land in different places.

No AI is the analytical starting place of the course, and a fully legitimate outcome of the analysis. No AI tends to fit when distinctiveness depends on decisions AI would homogenize or when skill development is the goal. One Diligence consideration worth raising: Be explicit about where the no AI boundary applies. Does it exclude AI from research, from reference gathering, from administrative tasks around the project? AI involvement can creep into stages the creator hasn't consciously analyzed. The boundary deserves explicit definition.

Consultant is the outcome where AI functions as a thinking partner or research aid. Brainstorming, idea testing, research summaries, generating questions the creator hasn't thought of. None of AI's direct output appears in the final piece. Three Diligence considerations. First, the line between AI input and AI output in the work. A creator who brainstorms with AI and drafts independently is at consultant. A creator who incorporates phrasings or framings from that brainstorm into the draft has moved into a different outcome.

Second, data privacy. AI as consultant often means putting working material into an AI tool. Drafts, client information, sensitive content. Know what your tool does with what you put into it. Third and hardest to see, AI tools have framings and tendencies, and creators who consult AI repeatedly can end up working within a framing AI provided without noticing. The most successful uses of consultant involve the creator bringing their own framing to the consultation, rather than asking AI to frame the problem first.

Tool Builder is the outcome where AI helps build a tool, script, or system the creator uses in their practice. The tool is the AI-assisted artifact. The final creative work is made with the tool, but contains no AI-generated material. Common uses: batch processing scripts, custom asset managers, research interfaces, template systems, tools specific to a creator's practice that off-the-shelf software doesn't handle well.

Two Diligence considerations. First, the tool versus the work. Disclosure and audience expectation questions land differently on each side of that separation. Know which side each artifact is on. Second, tools shape work. A tool that handles a step in your process is embedding decisions about how that step gets done. Diligence at tool builder includes asking whether the tool is automating something that genuinely doesn't need the creator's attention, or automating a decision that should stay with the creator.

Co-creator is the outcome where AI generates material that becomes part of the final work. The creator directs, selects, integrates, and takes responsibility for what the audience receives. Common uses: selecting from AI-generated text variations, layering AI imagery into a composition that Illustrator finishes, using AI-generated stems in a composed track, incorporating AI dialogue variants edited into a script.

Four Diligence considerations. Disclosure. AI in the final work raises disclosure questions in most contexts. Contracts, field norms, publication guidelines, and platform requirements may all apply. Know what your specific context calls for. IP. AI-generated material has unsettled legal status in many jurisdictions. Raise the questions before delivery, not after.

Quality verification. Apply your Discernment standards to AI-generated material the same way you would to anything you made directly. Lastly, watch for drift. Co-creator can quietly shift into a configuration where the creator is accepting whatever AI produces rather than directing it. The configurations that hold up have clear standards before generating, and the willingness to discard output that doesn't meet them.

Medium is the outcome where AI is part of the work's form. The audience interacts with AI as part of experiencing the work. Interactive characters, generative installations, AI-driven NPC systems, adaptive storytelling. The work couldn't exist without AI running.

Four Diligence considerations. System behavior. The creator is responsible for how the AI behaves when audiences encounter the work. The audience doesn't see your prompt, they see the output, and your configuration is where your direction lives. Failure modes. Test for edge cases because audiences will find them.

Disclosure. Audiences benefit from knowing what they're interacting with. Implicit deception raises ethical questions that diligence should address before release. And duration. Medium produces work that operates over time. A chatbot character keeps responding for months. An installation runs the length of an exhibition. The creator's responsibility continues for as long as the work is operating.

A brief return to the course's analytical starting place, which is to begin without AI use. The point of organizing the outcomes this way is to make the choices visible. A creator who knows that consultant and co-creator are different outcomes with different Diligence implications can make a different decision than a creator who treats AI involvement as a single thing. The roles are a vocabulary for the decisions, not a hierarchy among them. Remember, you're in the driver's seat, and your every decision about when and where to use AI is yours to make.


## Consultant

Consultant

What it is

AI advises you, but nothing it produces appears in the final piece.

Diligence focus

Watch that its framing does not quietly steer your choices, and verify any facts or claims it offers.


## Tool Builder

Tool Builder

What it is

AI helps build a tool or process you use to make the work, not the final piece itself.

Diligence focus

Confirm the tool does what you think it does, and that it is not shaping the work in ways you did not choose.


## Co-Creator

Co-Creator

What it is

AI-generated material appears in the final work, alongside your own.

Diligence focus

Disclosure, quality control, and ownership. You stand behind every part the audience encounters, including the parts AI made.

Rights & impact

Know the provenance of the AI-made parts and your right to use them, disclose the collaboration, and watch for styles or likenesses traceable to specific creators — and for who is represented in the result.


## Medium

Medium

What it is

AI generation is the medium: its output is substantially the final work itself.

Diligence focus

The highest bar for transparency and curation. Be explicit about sourcing and about your editorial role in what gets shown.

Rights & impact

The highest bar: be explicit about sourcing and licensing, check outputs against identifiable works or styles, and stay accountable to everyone the piece represents or reaches.
