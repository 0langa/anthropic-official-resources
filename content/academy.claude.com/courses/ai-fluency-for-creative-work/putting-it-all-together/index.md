### Your Personal AI Use Policy[](https://academy.claude.com/courses/ai-fluency-for-creative-work/putting-it-all-together)

Assemble everything you have built into one living policy. Decide the role AI plays across your modes of work, set your disclosure commitments by audience, name the triggers that will make you revisit it, then stress-test it against three real situations and sign it.

#### What you'll do

1. 1Decide the roles. Where and how AI fits across your modes and stages of work.
2. 2Set disclosure. How and when you tell each audience about AI's role.
3. 3Name revision triggers. What will make you revisit this policy.
4. 4Stress-test it. Answer three real situations using only what the policy says.
5. 5Sign and save. Sign, date, set the next review, and copy the policy into your own document.

Three questions anchor the policy: where AI fits and where it does not, what you disclose and to whom, and when you will revisit. There is no single position on AI use: you decide it at the level of mode and task.

#### Part IV: The stress test

Answer each scenario using only what your policy says. If the policy cannot answer all three, it is not finished: revise the relevant section and try again.

Scenario A

A client emails: "Quick question before we sign, do you use AI in your work?" Write your reply, drawn from your roles and disclosure sections.

Scenario B

A new tool launches that does your hardest stage startlingly well. What does your policy require you to do before adopting it?

Scenario C

You are two days from a deadline, behind, and a shortcut your policy forbids would close the gap. What does the policy say, and what is the reasoning you recorded for why?

If a scenario left you guessing, go back and sharpen the section it draws on.
