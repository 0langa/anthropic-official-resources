A human-agent team in action

The Lantern team is ready to kick off the launch of their health tracker app. At kickoff, Ravi clearly declares the north star: Launch the health tracker app by next Monday with 80% of users in the first week completing the onboarding flow.

Next, Ravi asks the agent to review their conversations over the last few weeks and propose the jobs required to hit the launch date.

People on the team then assign every job to humans and agents on the team.

The agent ensures every job is tracked transparently for the team. Agents work fast. Humans focus on judgment calls and key decisions.

The team gets into a rhythm. For example, when it comes to preparing launch materials, the agent drafts, Mara decides and refines, and the agent ships the final product.

Because agents know the north star, they can also suggest proactive activities that support the goal. For example, when the onboarding completion rate dropped on the second day of the launch, the agent completed a data analysis showing the drop off points for the team to discuss in their standup.

Having role clarity, a clear goal, and transparent progress tracking isn't new. But it's more important than ever when humans and agents are working side by side.

The Lantern team kicks off the launch of their health tracker app with a written north star, a roster that names people and agents, and work everyone can see. Because the agent knows the goal, it spots the day-two drop in onboarding completion and brings an analysis to standup.

The best human-agent teams have clear roles, a written north star, the right information access, and trust. While all of these things also support teams without agents, adding agents to the team makes them even more important. Because the rate of task completion on human-agent teams tends to be higher, the cost of misalignment and error is also higher. Setting the stage for success is critical.

## Key takeaways[](https://academy.claude.com/courses/building-effective-human-agent-teams/what-a-strong-team-looks-like)

- **Work in public and give agents broad context.** For an agent, if it isn't written down and accessible, it doesn't exist.
- **Every human and agent gets a defined role with the right tools for the job.** One roster, one set of artifacts, and one working space, with human judgment on the most important decisions.
- **Set a north star to make agents more proactive.** Humans always set it. An agent that knows the goal can bring the team useful work toward it.
- **Build trust over time.** Grant autonomy in proportion to demonstrated reliability, then expand it deliberately.
