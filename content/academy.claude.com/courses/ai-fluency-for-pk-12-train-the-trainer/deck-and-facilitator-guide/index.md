Why AI fluency

Demystifying AI fluency

MythDigital literacy and AI literacy are the same thing.

RealityDigital literacy is a broad understanding of technology. AI literacy goes further: understanding what AI is and how it works.

MythAI fluency and AI literacy are the same thing.

RealityAI literacy is understanding what AI is and how it works. AI fluency is translating that understanding into your own practice: using AI well in an ethical, efficient, and safe way.

MythYou need to be a tech expert to become AI fluent.

RealityAI fluency is built on professional judgement, critical thinking, domain knowledge, and your pedagogy, not technical expertise.

MythUsing AI well means using it as much as possible.

RealityKnowing when not to use AI is a core marker of fluency. More use does not equal more fluency.

Inner loop: discernment

Use the CHECK method

C**Correct?** Are the facts accurate? Verify claims with outside sources.

H**Human?** Does this feel warm and authentic?

E**Equitable?** Free of bias or cultural assumptions that could hurt students?

C**Curriculum-aligned?** Does it match my standards, objectives, grade level?

K**Kid-safe?** Appropriate and protective of student well-being?

Anthropic x AFT

The 4D Framework: the inner loop - Description & Discernment

Discernment matters because AI has limitations

**Why do AI models hallucinate?** Hallucinations occur when AI follows a logical pattern that isn’t grounded in reality.

**What is sycophancy?** Sycophancy happens when AI’s training makes it too helpful and likely to agree.

**Why does bias exist?** Bias happens when AI reproduces inequalities present in its training data.

PreK · Early childhood

Two exercises you can run tomorrow.

Exercise 1 · ~10 min

The read-aloud retelling

Ask AI to retell a familiar picture book three ways: for an English newcomer, with rhyme & rhythm, focused on one big feeling.

*Discern:* which one would actually work in morning circle, and why?

Exercise 2 · ~10 min

The student note translator

Draft a student update. Ask AI to rewrite it at three reading levels and in two home languages.

*Discern:* which version would you actually send, and what would you change first?

Elementary

Two exercises you can run tomorrow.

Exercise 1 · ~10 min

The misconception hunter

Pick a topic you are teaching this week. Ask AI for the five most common misconceptions students have, with examples of how each shows up in student work.

*Discern:* which have you actually seen, and which feel made up?

Exercise 2 · ~10 min

The academic content decoder

The 4D Framework: the outer loop - Delegation & Diligence

Activity: The Delegation Matrix

Delegation is deciding which tasks benefit from AI and which still need you. Sort each by **impact** and **AI risk**. 12 min, small groups.

HIGH Impact + LOW AI Risk

→ Delegate Fully

HIGH Impact + HIGH AI Risk

→ Delegate with Review

LOW Impact + LOW AI Risk

→ Delegate Occasionally

LOW Impact + HIGH AI Risk

→ Keep Human

Tasks to place:

- Writing a first-draft rubric
- Drafting a classroom newsletter
- Identifying students who need extra support
- Giving feedback on an essay draft
- Building relationships with students
- Planning a hard conversation

As a group, place each task in a quadrant, then add a few of your own. Discuss.

Outer loop: delegation

Some work is the work because you do it.

A reflective letter to a graduating senior. A hard conversation with a colleague. The thinking you do to understand why a kid is struggling. AI taking it from you does not save time: it removes the point.

Anthropic x AFT

Outer loop: diligence

Diligence, in practice

When your name is on it, you are accountable.

01

Verify

Check the facts, the reading level, anything that could mislead a student or caregiver.

02

Own

Revise it until it sounds like you and reflects your judgment, not the AI’s defaults.

03

Disclose

If AI helped produce it, say so. Transparency is part of the professional standard.

Anthropic x AFT

The 4D Framework: the outer loop - Delegation & Diligence

Protect Student data: FERPA, COPPA, and HIPAA

01

Use only district-approved tools.

If your district has a list, that’s the list. If they don’t, ask before you start.

02

Never paste anything that identifies a student.

Names, IEP details, demographics, behavioral notes. De-identify first: “Student 1, Student 2.”

03

Assume your inputs may be retained.

Different tools and plans have very different policies. Read them, or ask someone in your district.

The 4D Framework: the outer loop - Delegation & Diligence

PreK · Early childhood

Two exercises with the gates on.

Exercise 1 · ~15 min

The routine builder, with a delegation gate

Designing a new arrival routine. Before opening AI, list what AI can help with (visual schedule wording, a song) and what stays yours (which kid needs a soft landing).

*Diligence:* would you actually use this with three- and four-year-olds?

Exercise 2 · ~15 min

The home-communication audit

List the communication you send home in a month. Sort each into “AI can draft,” “AI structures, words stay mine,” and “AI does not touch.”

*Diligence:* defend your sort to a partner.

Anthropic x AFT

The 4D Framework: the outer loop - Delegation & Diligence

Elementary

Two exercises with the gates on.

Exercise 1 · ~15 min

The accommodation language draft

Pick a regular accommodation type (extended time, chunked text, sensory breaks). Ask AI to draft generic, de-identified language you could adapt later. Write down what you will not put in.

*Diligence:* does it match your district’s format? Would you sign your name to it?

Exercise 2 · ~15 min

The conference prep, no names

Pick an upcoming conference. Ask AI to build a structure and open-ended questions, with no student name and no identifying details.

*Diligence:* what did AI not know that only you do?

Anthropic x AFT

The 4D Framework: the outer loop - Delegation & Diligence

Middle school

Two exercises with the gates on.

Exercise 1 · ~15 min

The policy stress test

Pick a classroom policy you are considering (phones, late work, AI use itself). Ask AI to argue against it from three perspectives: a student, a caregiver, a colleague.

*Diligence:* are the arguments fair, or empty? Does it change what you would write?

Exercise 2 · ~15 min

The attendance check-in

Ask AI to write a series of student outreach letters for students who have missed school: a warm check-in, then a direct note about grades, then a formal notice of concern.

*Diligence:* what is AI missing? The log is the artifact.

The 4D Framework: the outer loop - Delegation & Diligence

High school

Two exercises with the gates on.

Exercise 1 · ~15 min

The disclosure draft

Take a piece of communication you would actually send that AI helped produce. Write a one-sentence disclosure you would be comfortable including. Does it change how the message reads? Should it?

*Diligence:* would you be comfortable signing your name to it?

Exercise 2 · ~15 min

An at-home conversation starter

Pick a single topic from class. Ask AI for five curious, open-ended conversation starters a caregiver could use at home, not quizzes. Write down what you will not put in.

*Diligence:* bring it back to share.
