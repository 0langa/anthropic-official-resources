## *1*More people asked for regular time with their manager than for anything else

Broad signalHighest in Engineering and Customer Support

**168 of 441 people who commented (38%)** · 211 comments · up from second place in Q1 (29%). Highest in Engineering (52% of its commenters) and Customer Support (47%). The managers these comments describe have a median of 9 direct reports; the company median is 6, and the roster shows 14 managers with more than 8.

*“I go three or four weeks between real 1:1s and everything gets saved up for the performance cycle.”* Engineering, 1–2 yrs

*“My manager is good at this but has eleven of us. It isn’t a skill problem.”* Customer Support, 2–4 yrs

*“Skip-levels stopped after the reorg and nobody said whether they’re coming back.”* Engineering, 4+ yrs

**30-day plan**Owner: VP Engineering and Head of Support, with their HRBPs

Broad signalEven across functions

*“Promotion criteria seem to depend on which team you’re in.”* Sales, 2–4 yrs

**30-day plan**Owner: People team (L&D) with function heads

Broad signalPresent in 7 of 9 functions

*“Half my week is other teams’ asks and there is no intake, so the loudest one wins.”* Design

*“I can’t tell which requests matter to the company and which matter to one person.”* Data

**30-day plan**Owner: COO’s office with Design and Data leads

## The prompt[](https://academy.claude.com/use-cases/engagement-survey-themes)

### Copy this into Claude Cowork[](https://academy.claude.com/use-cases/engagement-survey-themes)

Theme the open-text survey responses, segment by team and tenure where the data allows, and separate loud-minority comments from broad signal. Output a one-page People brief: the top three themes, two or three supporting quotes per theme, and a draft 30-day action plan for each. Never attribute a quote to an individual.

### Why this works[](https://academy.claude.com/use-cases/engagement-survey-themes)

Prompt

**State the privacy rule explicitly.** "Never attribute a quote to an individual" is a standing instruction in the skill; Cowork strips names, roles, and small-team identifiers so the brief is safe to share with leadership.

Prompt

**Say how to weight the responses.** "Loud-minority from broad signal" tells Cowork to weight a theme by how many distinct respondents raised it, not how forcefully a few wrote about it.

Prompt

**Ask for an action per theme.** Asking for "a draft 30-day action plan for each" turns the readout into something a leader can commit to in the meeting, not a finding to file.

Source

**Let the working folder supply context.** The raw export and the org roster sit in the working folder, so team and tenure cuts come from your real structure and the brief is saved next to the source data.

### Get a better draft[](https://academy.claude.com/use-cases/engagement-survey-themes)

Practice

**Add an example to match.** Drop an example you like into the folder and Cowork matches your structure and voice.

Practice

**Ask it to flag uncertainty.** Add "flag anything you're not confident about" so you know where to look first when you review the draft.
