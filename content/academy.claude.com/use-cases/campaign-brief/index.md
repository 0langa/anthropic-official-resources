## *1*Objective

Convert 1,500 workspaces to paid Harbor Shifts by 13 November 2026: 1,200 from existing payroll customers (4% of the 30,000 accounts with ten or more hourly employees) and 300 net-new. Secondary: 800 registrations across two partner webinars, and a pilot case study published by launch day.

## *2*Audience

Payroll administrators and operations managers at businesses with 20–200 mostly hourly employees — retail, clinics, restaurants — who already run payroll in Harbor or a comparable tool and build schedules somewhere else. Not enterprise this round.

## *3*Key message

Lead:*Hours go straight from the schedule to payroll — nobody retypes a timesheet.*

- 62% of surveyed admins re-enter hours by hand, averaging 3.4 hours per pay run (customer survey, June 2026, n = 418).
- Managers fill an open shift from the app in minutes, not a phone tree.
- Schedules go out two weeks ahead, which helps teams in cities with predictive-scheduling rules (legal-approved wording; we do not claim compliance).
- Proof asset: Miller’s Garden Centers pilot, 11 locations.

### Why this works[](https://academy.claude.com/use-cases/campaign-brief)

Prompt

**Name the sections.** Listing objective, audience, message, channels, timeline, and metrics up front means the brief follows your template, not a generic outline reviewers have to remap.

Prompt

**Ask for both deliverables at once.** "Build a short deck from the brief" gets you the kickoff artifact in the same pass, so the brief and the slides tell exactly the same story.

Source

**Point to your style guide.** "Consistent with our brand guidelines" points at the file in the folder, so headlines and copy come back sounding like you, not like a template.

Source

**Let the working folder supply context.** Strategy notes, template, and brand guide sit in the working folder, so the brief and deck are written back next to the source and the campaign folder is complete from day one.
