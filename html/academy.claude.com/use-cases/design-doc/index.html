The prompt

Copy this into Claude

Write a design doc for moving our event pipeline from batch to streaming. Look up prior art and architecture constraints, then draft the proposal in our RFC template: problem, goals and non-goals, two or three approaches with trade-offs, the recommendation, and open questions. I'll fill in the parts only I know.

Why this works

Prompt

Ask for prior art explicitly. Claude searches your wiki and past RFCs so the doc cites what's been tried before.

Prompt

Force two or three approaches. You get a real trade-off table, not a sales pitch for the first idea.

Prompt

Say which parts you'll fill in. Claude handles structure and lookup; you spend time on the actual judgment call.

Source

Give it your template to follow. Output follows your sections and headings, so reviewers see the structure they expect.

Get a better draft

Practice

Add an example to match. Drop an example you like into the folder and Claude matches your structure and voice.

Practice

Ask it to flag uncertainty. Add "flag anything you're not confident about" so you know where to look first when you review the draft.

Make Claude work for you

Tell Claude your own rules for this task, such as your prior-art sources and your reviewers' standing questions, then save what you've done as a skill of your own. A few minutes of conversation and it runs with your standards from then on.

Make it repeatable

Run it from any one-pager

Design docs start as a paragraph in a Slack thread or a notes file. Make an inbox folder inside your RFCs folder and save each new one-pager there. Ask Claude to set up a scheduled task or open Scheduled in the sidebar, and /rfc-draft turns any new one-pager in your RFCs/inbox folder into a structured first draft.

Set up a scheduled task for weekdays at 9am: check RFCs/inbox for new one-pagers, run /rfc-draft on each one, and write the structured draft to a numbered folder under RFCs/.

Share with your teammates

Your /rfc-draft skill now carries your template, your prior-art sources, and your reviewers' standing questions. Share it so every tech lead's RFC arrives in the same shape, and architecture review spends time on the decision instead of the formatting.



Share the skill

You can share a skill you created. In your skills(opens in new tab), open the ... menu next to /rfc-draft, select Share, and pick your teammates. For your whole organization, open the skill and select Publish to org, if your admin allows. They get the skill with your template and your reviewers' standing questions baked in, so they don't have to build it again.

What changes for design review

You have a complete RFC draft in your template, with prior art and trade-offs filled in from your own sources — ready to edit and review instead of write from a blank page.

You did this for one proposal. The same approach covers API changes, schema migrations, and architecture decisions — each one becomes a skill your team runs the same way.
