Five questions to assess your team's readiness for multiplayer AI

Before you implement multiplayer AI, it's good to consider how ready your team is. Here are five questions to get started.

One. Is what people and agents need open and searchable? If most of your team's work lives in private documents and DMs, it will be hard for agents to stay up to date.

Two. Can you write down who owns what, agents included? Without clear roles, work can be duplicated.

Three. Does every teammate have the tools for the job? An agent without access does thin work, guesses, or makes mistakes.

Four. Can key work done by an agent be checked before a person sees it? A rubric, a test, or a second agent reviewing can reduce the amount of time required to finalize an agent's work.

Five. Is there a north star everyone can point to? Without a clear goal, it's hard for humans and agents to be proactive about what's needed next.

Your prompt for Claude

I lead a team that is getting ready to work with AI agents in our shared spaces (multiplayer AI), and I want a practical plan. I just rated my team on five readiness statements, from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree):

  • - The information people and agents need is open and searchable, not kept in private documents and DMs. My rating: (not answered yet).
  • - We could write down who owns what on our team, agents included. My rating: (not answered yet).
  • - Every teammate, person or agent, has the tools and access their job needs. My rating: (not answered yet).
  • - Key work done by an agent can be checked with a rubric, a test, or a second agent before a person sees it. My rating: (not answered yet).
  • - We have a written north star everyone on the team can point to. My rating: (not answered yet).

Once I have rated all five, start with the areas I rated lowest. For each one, ask me two short questions about how my team works today, one at a time, then give me a two-week plan with one concrete change per area, who should own it, and how we would know it worked.

Key takeaways

  • Open and searchable. If most of your team's work lives in private documents and DMs, agents will struggle to stay up to date.
  • Clear owners, agents included. Without role clarity, work gets duplicated.
  • The right tools for every teammate. An agent without access does thin work, guesses, or makes mistakes.
  • A check before a person sees the work. A rubric, a test, or a second agent reviewing cuts the time it takes to finalize an agent's work.
  • A north star everyone can point to. Without a clear goal, neither people nor agents can be proactive about what's needed next.
